# Boss Counterplay Notes

## Scope

This note maps the first 16 real waves to their boss fantasy, the pressure they apply, and the player response the encounter is meant to teach.

## Waves 1-8

1. **Wave 1 - Commander**
   Teaches the player to respect a slow push. The boss should feel readable, deliberate, and backed by formation pressure.
   Player answer: stabilize the front and clear escorts before greedily adding more towers.

2. **Wave 2 - Hunter**
   Introduces constant repositioning pressure. The boss should make stationary play feel unsafe without becoming random.
   Player answer: keep moving laterally and let burst windows happen after the dive.

3. **Wave 3 - Fortress**
   First real endurance wall. The boss is not about surprise; it is about whether the build can keep a lane from collapsing.
   Player answer: slow it, chip it early, and keep splash damage ready for the support pack.

4. **Wave 4 - Prism**
   Shifts the test from raw stats to pattern reading. Crossed beams should force a short relocation rather than unavoidable damage.
   Player answer: leave intersections first, then re-enter the safe firing angle.

5. **Wave 5 - Hive**
   Introduces "board state snowball" pressure. The danger is not one hit, but letting the field become owned by spawners.
   Player answer: kill fresh nest points and deny growth before resuming boss damage.

6. **Wave 6 - Frost Judge**
   Makes spacing matter. The encounter should punish stacking all value into one frozen pocket.
   Player answer: split critical towers across zones and pre-move before the lock lands.

7. **Wave 7 - Rail Warlord**
   The first anti-clump boss. It should punish neat but overly linear tower setups.
   Player answer: spread priority towers and react to sight-lines immediately.

8. **Wave 8 - Collector**
   Adds economic pressure. The feeling should be "I am losing tempo" rather than simple HP loss.
   Player answer: protect income and stop repeated steal cycles, even if that means delaying greedy purchases.

## Waves 9-16

9. **Wave 9 - Twins**
   First true dual-boss exam. The encounter is about role recognition, crossfire awareness, and cleanup under enrage.
   Player answer: identify which twin is restricting movement, break the pair, then survive the empowered remainder.

10. **Wave 10 - Dragon**
    A stage-wide sweeper. The boss should feel like it is rewriting safe ground every few seconds.
    Player answer: keep a moving route, dodge laterally through breath lines, and only recommit after dives resolve.

11. **Wave 11 - Spider Matriarch**
    A territorial trap boss. The pressure comes from webs plus body-blocking adds, not pure burst.
    Player answer: cut open escape lanes first and never let webs and spiderlings stack uncontested.

12. **Wave 12 - Astrolabe**
    A displacement boss. Safe positions become unsafe because the fight changes where "center" effectively is.
    Player answer: preserve turning room and leave the gravity line before the singularity closes.

13. **Wave 13 - Blood Forge**
    A conversion boss. Small enemies are fuel, so the boss becomes more dangerous if the player tunnels the core target.
    Player answer: thin sacrifice targets before phase spikes and avoid feeding the forge a full wave.

14. **Wave 14 - Void Conductor**
    A rhythm boss. The encounter should feel memorable because repeated patterns become learnable beats.
    Player answer: move for the next beat early and treat grouped telegraphs as timing, not chaos.

15. **Wave 15 - Labyrinth Keeper**
    A routing boss. The key tension is being shepherded into bad geometry.
    Player answer: avoid dead-end tower clusters and always keep one horizontal escape line open.

16. **Wave 16 - Nightmare Bloom**
    A late-wave attrition boss. The arena should feel progressively contaminated if the player hesitates.
    Player answer: leave infected edges quickly and clear propagation nodes before the garden spreads.

## Use During Tuning

- If a boss kills the player without the note's intended lesson being visible, the encounter is too noisy.
- If the player can ignore the note's intended answer and still solve the fight by raw damage, the encounter is too flat.
- If two adjacent waves teach the same lesson in nearly the same way, change the enemy composition before raising numbers.
